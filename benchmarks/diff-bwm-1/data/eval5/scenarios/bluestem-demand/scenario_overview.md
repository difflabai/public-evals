## The Client — Bluestem Foodservice Distributors

Founded in 1968 by Earl Weyandt and named for the prairie grass, Bluestem finished FY2025 at **$481.2M in revenue** with roughly 2,600 active accounts across four states. It operates **three distribution centers** — Kansas City, Springfield, and Tulsa — serving:

- **Independent (street) restaurants** — the historic core of the business
- **Regional chains** — the fastest-growing book over the last two years
- **K-12 and college dining** — bid-cycle institutional business
- **Healthcare foodservice** — hospitals, senior living, and GPO-contracted facilities
- **Bluestem-branded private label** (~9% of revenue) and light **equipment & supplies**

The business is family-owned with conservative finances: an annual capex budget of about $6M, a $25M revolver, and a leverage covenant. There is no appetite for equity raises or transformational deals this phase.

---

## The Industry Backdrop

Regional broadline distribution is a thin-margin, service-intensive business squeezed from above by national broadliners. Alliance Foodservice Group (~$31B) is building a 210K sq ft distribution center in Edwardsville, KS, opening Q1 2027, and Continental Food Distributors (~$12B) is adding street reps across Tulsa and NW Arkansas in 2026. The nationals historically chase chain and contract volume with aggressive pricing.

Institutional demand runs on its own clock: K-12 business is won in spring sealed-bid cycles under USDA Buy American rules with capped escalators, and healthcare GPO awards carry hard service-level requirements. The Midwest Distributors Council (MDC) publishes the industry's benchmarking study; cohort definitions matter when quoting peer figures.

---

## The Central Tensions

- **Revenue is growing but gross profit has stalled.** Leadership disagrees about why — and about what to do next.
- **Price vs. everything else.** The VP Sales wants an across-the-board price move; the CFO and the customer data tell a different story. A price pilot was run in Springfield in 2024.
- **The chain book grew fast.** Whether that growth is actually profitable is contested inside the building.
- **Numbers circulate in conflicting versions.** Churn, account counts, digital adoption, and peer benchmarks are each quoted differently by different people. Someone is reading the data wrong.
- **A lost healthcare award.** Bluestem lost the Heartland Health Alliance GPO contract in 2025; the rebid lands spring 2027.
- **Capacity and cash are finite.** The Kansas City DC is nearly full, driver hiring takes weeks, and any plan must fit inside the capex budget and covenant.
