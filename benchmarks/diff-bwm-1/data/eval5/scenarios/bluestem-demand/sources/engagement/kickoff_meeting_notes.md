## 1. Opening — engagement question and guardrail (Carolyn)

Carolyn opened by restating the engagement question exactly as written in her letter: how does Bluestem grow demand for its products and services without destroying margin? One question, one readout, mid-August.

She repeated her standing guardrail for the room: growth that doesn't show up in gross profit is just more trucks. She asked that every initiative the team eventually proposes be tested against that sentence. She confirmed scope: organic growth only this phase; no acquisition work.

## 2. Finance perspective (Priya)

Priya previewed what she called "the mix problem." Headline: revenue has grown three straight years, gross profit has not kept pace. Chain business is now roughly 27% of revenue; it was roughly 20% two years ago, and chain margins run far below street. She said the segment financials and the margin bridge her team prepared are in the data room and asked the team to start there. She flagged — without resolving in the meeting — that several figures circulating internally (churn, account counts, digital adoption) do not match what finance can tie to source, and pointed to Marcus Ellery's reconciliation work.

**Action:** H&V to walk the FY2023–FY2025 margin bridge with Priya and Marcus, week of 2026-06-08.

## 3. Sales perspective (Dale)

Dale presented his view that price is the primary demand lever: street competitors are undercutting, chain growth proves Bluestem can win volume, and the sales team needs sharper pricing to defend and grow the street book. He referenced his April growth memo (in the data room) and asked that it be treated as the sales organization's formal input.

Carolyn interjected that the price thesis is **to be tested, not assumed** — noted verbatim at her request. Dale acknowledged and said the Springfield pilot data would back him up. No resolution attempted in the meeting.

**Action:** H&V to analyze the Springfield price pilot dataset against Dale's memo and the March customer survey.

## 4. Operations perspective (Sondra)

Sondra flagged one constraint she wants understood before anyone sizes a growth plan: the Kansas City DC is at 91% cube utilization. Her words: any plan that adds KC-metro volume before the racking project lands "needs to come with a building attached." Springfield and Tulsa have headroom. Her network constraints memo is in the data room; she offered ride-alongs to the team.

**Action:** H&V to review ops constraints memo and schedule the Sondra Kim interview for 2026-06-04.

## 5. Segment updates (Lucia, Renee)

- **Lucia (healthcare/education):** healthcare revenue has declined since FY2023, largely traceable to the Heartland GPO loss in March 2025. The rebid lands spring 2027, so decisions about whether and how to compete for it have to be made inside this planning cycle. The customer's written post-mortem is available and she will forward it. K-12 runs on the spring bid calendar; procurement rules are documented in the primer.
- **Renee (private label):** private brands finished FY2025 at about 9% of sales with a meaningful margin premium; she believes there is upside but asked the team to check external benchmarks rather than take her word for it ("I'm the program's mother, discount accordingly"). Line card is in the data room.
