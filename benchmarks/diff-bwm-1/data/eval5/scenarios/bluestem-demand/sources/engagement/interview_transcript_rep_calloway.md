[00:02:12] **INTERVIEWER:** So what keeps a customer, in your experience?

[00:02:15] **CALLOWAY:** Well, the save keeps them. But you want to know what loses them, and I'll tell you: price. Price is number one, two, and three with these customers. Everything else is decoration. Every week I'm out there getting beat over the head with somebody's cheaper case of fryer oil. Continental's got new reps crawling all over the metro handing out intro pricing like Halloween candy. A restaurant runs on nickels. You think Denny loves me so much he'll pay a dollar more a case forever? He will not.

[00:03:03] **CALLOWAY:** [pause] Yeah, well. The chicken's the marriage, the price is the divorce, how about that. Look — they stay for the service, sure, I'll give you that. But when they leave, it's a price sheet that starts the conversation. Some new rep walks in with a quote, and if the customer's happy, he shows it to me and I sharpen a few lines and we all move on. If he's *not* happy — if we shorted him twice that quarter — the quote's the excuse and he's gone. So maybe you're right that the shorting matters more. But the quote is what I see. I fight the quote.

Twenty-two percent of GP on my book. You cut price four points across the board, you're cutting my pay too, and half the reps in this building can do that math even if they won't say it in the sales meeting. So I'm not going to sit here and tell you the memo's wrong, Dale's forgotten more about this business than I know. But nobody asked the reps whether we want to sell a cheaper case for a skinnier commission.
