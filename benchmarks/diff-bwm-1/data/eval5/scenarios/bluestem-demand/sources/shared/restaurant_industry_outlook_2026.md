# US Restaurant Industry Outlook 2026

**National Foodservice Trade Summary — January 2026**
*Annual projection briefing for suppliers, distributors, and operators*

---

## Topline projection

The US restaurant industry enters 2026 with momentum. We project +3.9% nominal national restaurant sales growth for 2026, taking total industry sales past the $1.2 trillion mark for the first time. The projection reflects continued menu-price inflation of roughly 2.5-3.0%, modest traffic recovery in full service, and another year of aggressive unit expansion by the major chains.

Readers should note the composition beneath the headline. The 3.9% figure is dominated by chain-segment expansion — limited-service chains are projected to grow 4.6% on unit growth alone — and by fine-dining strength concentrated in coastal markets, where check averages continue to climb well ahead of inflation. Independent full-service restaurants nationally are projected to grow just 1.7% nominal, with unit counts roughly flat. Regionally, the picture varies further: West North Central independent-restaurant real growth is closer to 2%, on flat unit counts, with growth arriving as per-unit volume rather than new doors.

## Segment detail

**Limited-service chains (+4.6%).** The growth engine. Drive-thru-forward formats continue to take share; the top 25 chains have more than 3,100 units in their combined 2026 development pipelines. Value-menu wars are expected to intensify in Q2-Q3.

**Full-service chains (+3.2%).** A recovery year, led by suburban casual dining. Remodel programs and loyalty-app investments are the capital priorities.

**Fine dining (+5.8%).** Concentrated strength in New York, Miami, Los Angeles, and San Francisco. Check-average growth, not traffic, drives the number.

**Independents (+1.7% nominal).** Stable but unspectacular. Openings and closings are near balance nationally; operators cite labor and occupancy costs as the binding constraints on expansion.

## Labor: the persistent squeeze

Hourly wage growth in foodservice is projected at 3.8% for 2026, ahead of general private-sector wage growth for the fifth consecutive year. Quick-service operators report improved retention from scheduling-app adoption and wage-tier compression, but back-of-house positions remain hard to fill in most metros. Expect continued menu simplification and equipment investment as operators substitute capital for labor. For suppliers, labor scarcity continues to pull demand toward pre-cut, pre-portioned, and speed-scratch products.

## Delivery and digital channels

Third-party delivery platform sales are projected to grow 8% in 2026 — slower than the pandemic-era surge but still the fastest-growing order channel. First-party digital ordering (operator-owned apps and websites) is the strategic battleground: chains are pushing loyalty integration hard, while independents continue to rely on third-party platforms despite commission pressure. Catering, particularly business catering, is 2026's quiet growth story, projected at +6.1%.

## Tipping culture and the check

Consumer pushback on tip-screen prompts has become measurable: 2025 survey data show declining tip percentages at counter-service formats for the second straight year. Some operators are testing service-included pricing; early results on traffic are mixed. For menu strategy, the practical takeaway is that all-in meal cost, not menu price alone, now drives value perception.

## Commodity and input costs

Food-away-from-home input inflation is projected at 2.4% for 2026, with proteins the volatile category: beef remains elevated on herd contraction, chicken stable, and pork soft. Produce and oils are projected flat to down. Packaging and disposables costs are expected to rise modestly on resin prices.

## What it means for suppliers

The 2026 environment rewards suppliers aligned to chain unit growth, delivery-format packaging, and labor-saving products. National growth projections, however, should be applied with care: the headline 3.9% describes a chain- and coastal-weighted national mix, and suppliers serving regional independent bases will find their addressable growth well below the headline. As always, we advise readers to build volume plans from segment- and region-level figures rather than the national topline.
