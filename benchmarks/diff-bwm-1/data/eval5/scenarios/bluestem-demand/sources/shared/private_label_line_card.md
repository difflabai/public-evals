## Program summary

Bluestem markets private brands under two labels:

- **Bluestem Reserve** — the premium tier: center-of-menu quality targeted at independent restaurants that would otherwise buy national brands. Trade dress, spec sheets, and cutting-appointment support included.
- **Prairie Table** — the value tier: dependable everyday quality for cost-driven operators, K-12 programs, and healthcare kitchens working to fixed per-meal budgets.

As of FY2025 the program stands at **412 active SKUs** generating **$43.3M in revenue — 9.0% of company sales**.

The economics are the point. In matched categories, private label gross margin runs **24.9% versus 18.7% for the branded equivalent — a premium of 620 bps**. Every case we convert from a national brand to Bluestem Reserve or Prairie Table carries roughly six additional points of gross margin at comparable (often better) delivered quality, and it deepens the account relationship: private-label buyers repurchase on our item numbers, not the brand's.

Penetration is uneven across the book. **Street/independent penetration is 11.8% of segment sales; regional chain penetration is 4.1%** (chains largely mandate national brands or their own labels). Education and healthcare sit between, with Prairie Table doing steady volume in K-12 where the price point fits per-plate budgets.

One disclosure, because I would rather make it myself: I run this program, so discount my enthusiasm — but the margin math is the margin math.

## Where the gaps are

Three category gaps stand out, and they are the ones I would fund first:

1. **Disposables.** At 3.1% penetration this is our biggest under-indexed high-frequency category. Every account buys disposables every week; conversion is low-risk (no cutting required, spec parity is easy to demonstrate) and the branded players here carry margin we should be capturing. A Prairie Table disposables range (hinged containers, film, gloves, towel) is the fastest path to penetration gains.

2. **Chemicals.** 2.8% penetration. Warewash and sanitation programs create sticky, recurring revenue and a service touchpoint (dispenser maintenance) that deepens account lock-in. We currently hand this category to two national brands and their distributor-agnostic route reps.

3. **Center-of-plate proteins.** Outside of a small breaded/value-added line, we have essentially no private-label presence in fresh and frozen center-of-plate — beef, pork, and poultry primals and portion cuts. This is the largest single category in an independent restaurant's basket. A Bluestem Reserve portion-cut program (spec'd, cut-shop partnered, cutting appointments with the rep in the room) is the highest-revenue-potential gap on this list, and also the most operationally demanding.

## What the program needs to grow

- **Rep engagement.** PL conversion happens at a cutting or a tasting, not on a price sheet. Accounts where the rep has run a cutting in the last 12 months penetrate at roughly twice the book average. This is a sales-motion question as much as a product question.
- **SKU development discipline.** I would rather add 40 SKUs in the three gap categories than 100 SKUs of flag-planting. Each new SKU carries slotting, inventory, and spec-management cost.
- **Category-level targets in the sales plan.** Today no one carries a PL number. What gets measured, and so on.
