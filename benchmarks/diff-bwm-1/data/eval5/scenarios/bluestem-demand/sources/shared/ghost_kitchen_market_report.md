# The Ghost Kitchen Opportunity: 2026 National Outlook

**FoodChannel Insights — Special Report, February 2026**
*The essential briefing for foodservice suppliers who don't want to miss the decade's fastest-moving channel*

---

## The headline: 18% and accelerating

Ghost kitchens — delivery-only food production facilities operating without dine-in service — are no longer an experiment. They are the fastest-growing physical format in American foodservice, and our 2026 outlook projects the channel growing at 18% per year through 2029. While traditional restaurant formats fight for low-single-digit growth, ghost kitchens are minting new demand for food, packaging, and supplies at a pace the industry has not seen since the fast-casual boom.

Our model puts the national ghost-kitchen food-and-supplies purchasing pool at $3.4B in 2026, on a path to exceed $6.5B by 2030. For distributors, this is a customer base that did not exist a decade ago — and one with no legacy supplier loyalties. Every facility that opens is an open account waiting to be won.

## Why the format wins

Three structural forces power the channel:

1. **Delivery demand is sticky.** Consumers who built delivery habits between 2020 and 2023 have kept them. Third-party platform order volume grew again in 2025, and virtual brands let operators multiply menu concepts out of one kitchen.
2. **The economics of density.** A single 8,000 sq ft ghost-kitchen facility can house 15-25 operator stalls, each running 1-3 virtual brands. One loading dock, one receiving process, many customers — a distributor's dream drop.
3. **Capital keeps coming.** Despite well-publicized stumbles by early operators, 2025 saw fresh institutional investment in second-generation facility models with stronger unit economics.

## Case study: Los Angeles

Los Angeles remains the nation's ghost-kitchen capital, with more than 400 tracked facilities. One mid-sized LA distributor we interviewed built a dedicated ghost-kitchen route structure in 2024 and reports the channel now contributes 7% of its revenue, with average weekly drops 2.3x the size of its typical street account. "It's the easiest route my drivers run," the company's sales director told us. "Twenty customers, one dock."

## Case study: Chicago

Chicago's cloud-kitchen cluster along the near west side has become a proving ground for virtual-brand licensing. A West Loop facility we toured houses 22 operators producing for 61 virtual brands. Distributors serving the facility report packaging and disposables volume per operator running well above traditional QSR benchmarks — a margin-friendly mix.

## What suppliers should do now

- **Track facility openings, not restaurant permits.** Ghost kitchens don't show up in traditional new-restaurant data.
- **Sell the stall, not the building.** Each operator stall is a separate buying decision; facility-level master agreements remain rare.
- **Lead with packaging and disposables.** They are the channel's fastest-growing categories and the easiest first order.

The window matters. In every metro we track, the first distributor to build a ghost-kitchen-specific service model captured a disproportionate share of the channel and has held it.
