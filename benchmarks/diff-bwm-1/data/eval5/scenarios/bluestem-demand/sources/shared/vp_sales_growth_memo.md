# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Executive Leadership Team
**From:** Dale Hutmacher, VP Sales
**Date:** 2026-04-21
**Re:** Growth Acceleration Memo — How We Win the Next $100M

---

Team,

I've been selling in this market for twenty-four years, and I'll tell you what I told Carolyn last week: we are sitting on the biggest growth opportunity this company has seen since we opened Tulsa, and the only thing standing between us and it is our own timidity on price.

Let me lay it out.

## 1. The market is telling us something. Listen to it.

Our CRM shows customer churn running at 19%, which is exactly why we need to get more aggressive on price before we bleed out. Nearly one in five customers walking out the door every year is not a service problem, it is not a technology problem, it is a price problem. I hear it from my reps every single week: Continental and Alliance are buying our accounts out from under us with pencil-sharpened quotes, and we respond with a price book that hasn't gotten meaningfully more aggressive in three years.

We proudly serve over 3,100 customers across this region. Every one of them gets a competitor's quote in front of them at least once a quarter. If we want to keep them — and take their neighbors — we have to be in the fight on price.

## 2. Chains prove we can win when we compete.

Look at what happened when we actually decided to compete for volume. The chain business is our growth engine. We have grown it 38% in two years and it is profitable growth — every case we ship a chain is a case our competitors don't. That's $92.8M to $128.4M in twenty-four months. No other part of our book has grown like that, because no other part of our book has been allowed to compete on price the way our national accounts team competes.

That is what winning looks like. Trucks full, docks busy, revenue climbing. I want the same aggressiveness applied to the street.

## 3. We already ran the experiment. It worked.

Some people in this building will tell you price doesn't move volume. The data says otherwise. When we tested sharper pricing in Springfield, volume responded immediately — proof that price moves the needle. Our reps in that market went from playing defense to writing new orders. Ask any of them which price book they'd rather carry.

## 4. Where we keep losing — and why.

Heartland. I know it's a sore subject, but let's be honest about what happened. We lost the Heartland GPO award on price, plain and simple. It will keep happening until we sharpen pencils. That was $14M+ of annual volume that walked to a competitor, and every bid we take to a GPO or a school district from here forward is going to end the same way if we show up with the same numbers.

And before anyone brings up the portal: over half our street customers are already comfortable ordering from us however they want to order. Technology is not what wins the account. The rep wins the account, and the rep wins it on price and relationship. Let's not spend a year and a million dollars solving a problem we don't have while Continental hires reps in Tulsa.

## 5. The proposal.

Here is what I'm asking the ELT to approve for FY2027 planning:

1. **A 4–6% across-the-board price reduction on the street book.** Funded partly by volume recovery and partly by vendor negotiation. This is how we stop the churn and win back share. Our reps need a price book they can sell from, not apologize for.

2. **Double our chain bid activity.** We won 38% growth in two years by showing up aggressively. There are at least six regional chain RFPs in the next twelve months where we currently plan to no-bid or bid soft. Bid them all, bid them hard.

3. **Re-bid Heartland in 2027 with a price-first package.** Get the pencil out early. I want our healthcare pricing reviewed line by line before that RFP drops.

4. **Rep headcount protection.** Whatever we do on technology, we do not touch rep commission economics. My senior guys are the reason customers stay, and my senior guys are being recruited every month. (Ask Hoss Calloway what Continental offered him in March.)

## 6. What I need.

I need finance to stop treating every price proposal like a margin funeral and start treating it like an investment in share. Share is what compounds. Margin follows volume in this business — always has. My twenty-four years on the street are the dataset.

Give my team a competitive price book and the freedom to use it, and I will deliver double-digit street growth by this time next year.

Happy to walk any of this at the ELT meeting. I'll bring the coffee, you bring the approvals.

Dale

*Dale Hutmacher | VP Sales, Bluestem Foodservice Distributors | "The order goes to whoever shows up."*
