# Harrow & Vance — Research Desk
## Competitive Landscape Brief: Bluestem Foodservice Trade Area

**Date:** 2026-05-30
**Prepared for:** Bluestem engagement team (E. Sorrell)
**Prepared by:** H&V Research Desk, Chicago
**Sources:** Public filings, trade press, permit and property records, MDC channel commentary, and interviews with two former employees of the companies profiled. Confidence levels noted per item.

---

### 1. Purpose

This brief summarizes competitive developments in Bluestem's four-state trade area (Kansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, northwest Arkansas) that bear on the demand-growth question. It covers two confirmed moves by national broadliners, one unverified market rumor, and the pattern implications for FY2027 planning.

### 2. Alliance Foodservice Group — Kansas City metro entry (confirmed, high confidence)

Alliance Foodservice Group (national broadliner, ~$31B in revenue) announced in March 2026 the construction of a 210K sq ft distribution center in Edwardsville, KS, with an announced opening in Q1 2027. The announcement was covered in trade press and is corroborated by Wyandotte County permit filings and a state incentive award.

Assessment: Alliance does not build forward capacity speculatively. A facility of this size implies a targeted revenue load in the $250M-$400M range for the KC metro and surrounding lanes. Based on Alliance's entry behavior in three comparable metros since 2021 (Columbus, Des Moines, Boise), the playbook is consistent: aggressive introductory pricing on chain and institutional contract volume in the first 18-24 months, staffed by a small street-sales team added later. Two of Bluestem's regional-chain customers are of a size and profile that would appear on Alliance's target list.

### 3. Continental Food Distributors — Tulsa / NW Arkansas street push (confirmed, moderate-high confidence)

Continental Food Distributors (national broadliner, ~$12B in revenue) is adding 12 street sales representatives across Tulsa and northwest Arkansas during 2026, per job postings tracked since January and confirmation from a former Continental regional manager. Continental serves the area today from its Oklahoma City facility; the hiring pattern suggests a share-taking push into independent restaurants rather than new-facility groundwork.

Assessment: 12 reps at a typical mature book of $2.0M-$3.5M each implies Continental is targeting $25M-$40M of street volume in the corridor over 2-3 years. Continental's street pricing has historically been undisciplined during land-grab phases, then reverts within 24 months.

### 4. Market chatter: Prairie Star Foods (unverified — do not rely)

We are aware of chatter, relayed through Bluestem sales channels, that Prairie Star Foods is in financial difficulty and may exit the Tulsa market. We ran this to ground: Prairie Star's credit profile shows no public distress markers, its two largest suppliers report normal payment behavior, and its Tulsa facility lease was renewed in 2025. We found no corroboration for market chatter that Prairie Star Foods intends to exit the Tulsa market; treat as unverified. Any initiative sized on capturing Prairie Star volume would be sized on a single-source rumor, and we recommend the engagement exclude it from the base plan.

### 5. Pattern analysis

Both confirmed movers are national broadliners running the same historical sequence: (1) buy share on chain and contract volume with price, absorbing thin or negative margins on entry; (2) densify routes; (3) restore pricing once volume commitments lock in. Neither has historically sustained a service advantage with independent street accounts in contested regional markets — their fill rates and delivery-window flexibility for small-drop customers lag the regional incumbents they attack. In the three comparable metro entries reviewed, the regional incumbent that defended successfully did so on street service intensity and category support, not by matching contract pricing.

### 6. Implications for the engagement

- **Chain price pressure will intensify into FY2027.** Alliance's Edwardsville opening lands Q1 2027 and its early bids will target exactly the volume Bluestem's chain book represents. Competing for that volume on price would compound the margin problem the engagement was retained to solve.
- **The street and institutional service position is the defensible ground.** Both entrants are structurally weakest where service intensity matters most: independent accounts, small drops, tight delivery windows, and compliance-heavy institutional business.
- **Timing matters.** Bluestem has roughly three quarters before Alliance is operational. Initiatives that deepen street account stickiness and institutional service levels ahead of Q1 2027 are worth materially more than the same initiatives started after.
