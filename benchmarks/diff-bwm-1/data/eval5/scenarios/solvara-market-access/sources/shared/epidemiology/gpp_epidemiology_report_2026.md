# US Epidemiology of Generalized Pustular Psoriasis (GPP), 2026

**Commissioned epidemiology report — Solvara (velankibart) launch planning**
**Prepared for:** Veloxa Therapeutics, Inc.
**Author:** Dr. Priya Anand, PhD, Epidemiologist
**Date:** 2026-05

---

## 1. Executive summary

This report estimates the prevalent, diagnosed US population of generalized pustular psoriasis (GPP), characterizes its age and sex distribution, and — importantly for launch planning — draws a careful distinction between the *diagnosed* population and the smaller *addressable* population of patients who are realistic candidates for a new subcutaneous maintenance biologic. The headline number practitioners should carry from this report is the diagnosed prevalent count; it should not be read as a treatable or candidate count, and it should certainly not be inflated beyond its stated range.

**US diagnosed prevalent GPP is estimated at approximately 7,500 patients (range 6,800-8,200).**

That figure is the diagnosed prevalence. The share of those patients who are appropriate candidates for a new self-administered maintenance biologic is materially smaller and is discussed in Section 5; sizing the true launch opportunity requires combining this report with treated-fraction data from claims and clinical candidacy input from payer and clinical advisors.

## 2. Methods

Estimates were built from a triangulated approach rather than a single data source, because GPP is rare and no single US registry captures it completely:

- **Claims-based case-finding.** We identified patients carrying an ICD-10-CM diagnosis of L40.1 (generalized pustular psoriasis) across a large multi-payer administrative claims database spanning commercial, Medicare, and Medicaid lives for calendar years 2023–2025, applying a two-claim requirement to reduce rule-out coding.
- **Published prevalence reference points.** We calibrated against peer-reviewed US and international prevalence estimates for GPP, which cluster in the range of roughly 2 to 12 cases per million under registry and confirmed-case definitions, and run materially higher — up to the low twenties per million — in claims-based US analyses that include probable cases. Our diagnosed estimate of ~7,500 (≈22 per million) is a claims-ascertainment figure and sits at that end of the spectrum by construction; a registry-style definition would put the US figure several-fold lower.
- **Coding-adjustment review.** Because GPP is frequently miscoded — most commonly to L40.0 (psoriasis vulgaris) — we performed a chart-pattern adjustment to account for both under-capture (true GPP coded as something else) and over-capture (non-GPP coded as L40.1 in error).

Uncertainty is genuine here. The range of 6,800 to 8,200 reflects sensitivity to case definition and to the coding adjustment, and it should be reported as a range, not a point estimate presented with false precision.

## 3. Prevalence

Applying the case definition and coding adjustment to the US population yields an estimated diagnosed prevalent GPP population of approximately 7,500 patients, with a plausible range of 6,800 to 8,200. GPP is a rare disease; even at the top of the range it is a small, concentrated population managed disproportionately by academic and referral dermatology centers.

We stress that this is *diagnosed* prevalence — patients who carry a GPP diagnosis in the system in the analysis period. It is not incidence (new flares per year), and it is not the number of patients who at any given moment are appropriate for, or actively seeking, a new maintenance biologic.

## 4. Age and sex distribution

GPP presents across the adult age span, with the diagnosed population skewed toward working-age and older adults. Pediatric GPP exists but is uncommon in the diagnosed prevalent pool. The age distribution matters for launch planning because it drives the expected payer mix: the older tail of the distribution is the basis for a meaningful Medicare share.

|---|---|---|
| 0–17 | 300 | 4.0% |

Just under a quarter of diagnosed patients (23%) are aged 65 or older. Not all of those are Medicare-primary (some remain on employer commercial coverage), and a portion of the under-65 population qualifies for Medicare on the basis of disability; netted together, the age structure supports a Medicare share in the low-twenties-percent range of treated lives and a commercial-majority mix among the remainder. Sex distribution is approximately balanced, with a modest female predominance in some series; it is not a material driver of access planning.

## 5. Diagnosed vs. addressable — a caution

This is the most important interpretive point in the report, and the one most often mishandled in launch materials.

**The diagnosed prevalent count (~7,500) is not the addressable/candidate count.** Several factors reduce the diagnosed pool to the population realistically appropriate for a new subcutaneous maintenance biologic:

- **Acute vs. maintenance status.** A share of diagnosed patients are captured in the context of an acute flare managed in the hospital or urgent-care setting, not the inter-flare maintenance setting Solvara is designed for.
- **Comorbidity and contraindication.** Some patients carry comorbidities or infection risk that make a systemic biologic inappropriate.
- **Prior-therapy and treatment-intent status.** Some diagnosed patients are stable on existing therapy, are not treatment-seeking, or are managed with topical/conventional approaches by preference.

The size of the candidate fraction is a clinical and payer-judgment question that this report does not resolve on its own. It should be set by combining the treated-fraction signal from the claims extract (what share of GPP-coded patients actually carry a systemic or biologic claim) with candidacy input from the clinical and payer advisory board. This report deliberately does **not** publish an addressable number; doing so would overstate the certainty of a figure that depends on those other inputs. Analysts sizing the opportunity should triangulate this diagnosed count with those sources rather than treating 7,500 as the market — and they should be especially wary of any planning figure that exceeds the diagnosed count itself.

## 6. Coding note

Correct disease coding materially affects both epidemiologic capture and downstream coverage. Generalized pustular psoriasis codes to **ICD-10-CM L40.1**. The most common miscode is **L40.0 (psoriasis vulgaris)**, a distinct and far more common disease. Miscoding to L40.0 both understates true GPP prevalence in claims analyses and, at the point of care, is a recognized driver of prior-authorization denial. Case-finding and benefits verification alike should confirm L40.1.

## 7. Limitations

Rare-disease epidemiology from administrative claims is sensitive to ascertainment. The estimate assumes the coding-adjustment factors derived from chart-pattern review generalize to the full US population; if GPP is more systematically miscoded than assumed, true prevalence could sit toward the upper bound. A further limitation is that administrative claims capture diagnosed and coded patients only: patients whose GPP is documented in narrative but coded to a non-GPP entity, and patients who are undiagnosed or newly diagnosed, are not fully represented. The estimate is a prevalent, diagnosed count for planning use and should be refreshed as real-world diagnosis and treatment patterns for Solvara emerge post-launch. Analysts should carry the diagnosed count with its stated range and resist the temptation to round it up or to treat it as the treatable market.
