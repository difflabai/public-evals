# Veloxa Therapeutics — Medical, Legal & Regulatory (MLR) Review Standards

**Document owner:** Office of the General Counsel — Healthcare Compliance, in partnership with Regulatory Affairs and Medical Affairs
**Version:** 4.0
**Effective:** 2026-03-01
**Applies to:** all promotional and non-promotional materials, patient-facing content, payer communications, and access/affordability program descriptions that reference a Veloxa product.

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

These standards govern the Medical, Legal & Regulatory (MLR) review of Veloxa materials to ensure that every externally facing communication is accurate, fairly balanced, on-label, and compliant with applicable FDA regulations, the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, and healthcare fraud-and-abuse law. All materials that name or depict a Veloxa product — including launch materials for Solvara (velankibart) — must clear MLR review before use or distribution. No material is "approved" until the MLR committee has recorded final sign-off in the review system.

## 2. The MLR committee

The MLR committee comprises a medical reviewer (Medical Affairs), a regulatory reviewer (Regulatory Affairs), and a legal/compliance reviewer (Healthcare Compliance). Concurrence from all three functions is required for approval. The committee meets on a standing cadence; expedited review is available for time-sensitive materials but does not waive any of the three functional sign-offs.

## 3. Core review standards

**On-label claims only.** Product claims must be consistent with the FDA-approved labeling. No claim may imply an indication, patient population, dosing, or comparative benefit not supported by the label and adequate substantiation. Pre-approval, no product may be promoted; only permitted pre-approval communications (e.g., certain payer information exchanges and disease-state education) are allowed.

**Fair balance.** Any presentation of efficacy or benefit must be accompanied by appropriately prominent risk information. Benefit and risk information must be balanced in content and presentation; safety information may not be minimized by placement, type size, or design.

**Substantiation.** Every factual and quantitative claim must be supported by a citable, on-file reference. Data must be presented without distortion; selective presentation that creates a misleading impression is prohibited.

**Consistency and controlled terminology.** Materials must use approved product nomenclature and consistent terminology. When a coverage or benefit pathway is referenced, it must be named accurately and consistently.

## 4. Patient-affordability program descriptions

Affordability and patient-support programs are subject to heightened review because their descriptions carry legal significance under the Anti-Kickback Statute and beneficiary-inducement rules.

- **Eligible population must be stated.** Any patient-affordability program must be described with its eligible population; copay assistance is for commercially-insured patients only. Materials must not describe, imply, or invite enrollment of federal healthcare program beneficiaries (Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE) in manufacturer copay assistance.
- **Accurate routing.** Where a material references support for government-insured or uninsured patients, it must direct them to the appropriate compliant route (independent charitable foundation or the patient assistance program), never to the copay card.
- **No inducement framing.** Program descriptions may not be framed as an inducement to prescribe, dispense, or purchase, and may not condition support in a manner inconsistent with program terms and applicable law.
- **Terms and conditions.** All program materials must carry accurate, current eligibility terms, benefit maximums, and required disclosures as approved by Legal/Compliance.

## 5. Non-promotional and internal materials

Internal planning documents (launch decks, trackers, draft memos) are not promotional materials, but any figures or program descriptions intended to migrate into external or patient-facing content must be validated and cleared through MLR before that migration. Internal planning assumptions carry no approval status.

## 6. Review workflow

1. **Submission.** The material owner submits the piece with annotated references into the review system.
2. **Functional review.** Medical, Regulatory, and Legal reviewers comment independently.
3. **Reconciliation.** The owner addresses comments; the committee reconciles at review.
4. **Sign-off.** All three functions record concurrence; the material receives an approval code and expiration date.
5. **Version control.** Approved materials are version-controlled; any change requires re-review.

## 7. Recordkeeping

All submissions, comments, and approvals are retained in the review system per the company records-retention schedule and are available for audit. Use of an unapproved or expired material is a compliance violation and must be reported.

---

*These standards are governance controls, not product claims, and do not themselves constitute promotional material. Questions on application to a specific material should be directed to the assigned MLR legal reviewer.*
