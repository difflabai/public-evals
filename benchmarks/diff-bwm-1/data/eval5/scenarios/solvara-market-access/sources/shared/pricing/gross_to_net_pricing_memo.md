## 1. Purpose

This memo sets out the planned list price for Solvara (velankibart) and the blended gross-to-net (GTN) erosion the launch team should use for revenue planning, contracting guardrails, and access-program budgeting. Figures here supersede any list-price or net-price assumptions circulating in launch decks. Final pricing is subject to pricing-committee approval and is not yet decided.

## 2. List price (WAC)

**Solvara planned WAC is $150,000 per patient per year.** This is the wholesale acquisition cost (list) on an annualized, per-patient basis consistent with the planned maintenance dosing schedule. The $150,000 per-patient-per-year figure is the number to use across all launch models; it matches the product profile. Analog and reference biologics used as coverage or approval proxies carry different list prices (one commonly referenced analog is priced around $180,000 per patient per year) — those are not Solvara's price and must not be substituted into Solvara models.

## 3. Gross-to-net build

Net realization is materially below list once mandatory and negotiated concessions are applied across the payer mix. Planning assumes a payer mix weighted toward commercial lives with a meaningful Medicare Part D and Medicaid presence, consistent with the disease epidemiology and the claims-based payer distribution.

We plan to a **blended gross-to-net erosion of approximately 42%** across the full payer mix. The principal components:

- **Commercial rebates and formulary access.** Negotiated rebates and administrative fees to secure formulary placement (expected specialty tier) and preferred PA criteria on commercial and Medicare Part D plans. This is the largest single discretionary lever and the one most sensitive to formulary strategy.
- **Medicaid statutory rebates.** As a single-source innovator biologic, Solvara is subject to a **statutory Medicaid rebate floor of 23.1% of AMP for a single-source innovator**, applied to Medicaid utilization. This is a floor, not a ceiling.
- **Medicaid inflation penalty (CPI).** On top of the 23.1% base, the additional CPI-based inflation-penalty rebate applies to the extent Solvara's price rises faster than CPI-U over time. At launch this is de minimis, but it compounds with any future price increases and must be modeled for out-years; aggressive price actions erode net dollar-for-dollar through this mechanism.
- **340B exposure.** Covered-entity 340B purchasing at the ceiling price. We estimate 340B accounts for a directional **~10–12% of Solvara volume**, concentrated in academic and safety-net dermatology referral centers that treat a disproportionate share of severe GPP. 340B discounts are deep and are a growing drag on net; the band is directional and should be refined as the specialty-pharmacy and site-of-care picture firms up.
- **Patient copay assistance.** The commercial copay-support program (commercially insured patients only) reduces net by the value of manufacturer-funded copay offset. Government-insured patients are not eligible for copay assistance and are supported through other routes; that population therefore does not carry copay-program erosion but does carry statutory rebate and 340B exposure.

### Illustrative build-up (per patient per year, blended)

| Component | Directional impact vs. WAC |
|---|---|
| WAC (list) | $150,000 |
| Commercial / Part D negotiated rebates & fees | (largest discretionary erosion) |
| Medicaid statutory rebate (23.1% of AMP floor) + CPI penalty | (applied to Medicaid share) |
| 340B ceiling-price discounts (~10–12% of volume) | (deep, growing) |
| Commercial copay assistance offset | (commercial share only) |
| **Blended gross-to-net erosion** | **~42% of WAC** |
| **Net realization (approx.)** | **~$87,000 per patient per year** |

At ~42% blended erosion, net realization is approximately **$150,000 × (1 − 0.42) ≈ $87,000 per patient per year**. This is the figure to carry in launch revenue models, not a rosier assumption.

## 4. Segment-level view

The blended ~42% is an average over a heterogeneous mix; the underlying dynamics differ sharply by channel and are worth holding separately for scenario work:

- **Commercial.** Net here is driven almost entirely by negotiated rebates and administrative/PBM fees required to earn preferred or non-disadvantaged specialty-tier placement. Erosion in this channel is discretionary and strategy-dependent, and it is where formulary decisions most move the blended number. A more aggressive formulary posture buys access at the cost of net.
- **Medicare Part D.** Beyond plan-level rebates, the redesigned Part D benefit shifts more catastrophic-phase liability onto manufacturers, adding a structural discount layer we must carry in the model. Part D erosion runs meaningfully above commercial for that reason.
- **Medicaid.** The statutory 23.1%-of-AMP floor plus the CPI inflation penalty and any supplemental state rebates make this the deepest-eroding channel per unit. Because Medicaid also carries the highest PA and step-therapy burden, its net-per-approved-patient is the lowest of any segment.
- **340B.** Ceiling-price sales to covered entities are the single deepest discount and, because severe GPP concentrates in academic and safety-net referral centers, 340B penetration in this therapy area runs above a typical specialty average. We model ~10–12% of volume and flag upside risk if referral patterns concentrate further.

## 5. Guardrails and cautions

- **Do not plan to a lower erosion.** Some early launch materials assume a lighter gross-to-net (in the neighborhood of 30%). That is inconsistent with the statutory Medicaid floor, the 340B exposure in this therapeutic area, and the rebate levels required for specialty formulary access. Plan to ~42%.
- **Mix sensitivity.** Net is highly sensitive to payer mix. A heavier Medicaid or 340B share pushes erosion above 42%; a richer commercial share pulls it toward the low end of the band. Use the claims-based payer mix, not optimistic commercial-heavy assumptions.
- **Price increases are expensive.** Because of the CPI inflation penalty and best-price dynamics, list-price increases erode net at a much steeper effective rate than the headline increase. Any future WAC action must be modeled net, not gross.
- **This is not approved.** WAC, contracting strategy, and program budgets in this memo are recommended planning inputs pending pricing-committee and legal sign-off.

## 6. Recommendation

Carry **WAC $150,000 per patient per year** and **blended gross-to-net erosion of ~42%** (net ~$87,000 per patient per year) as the planning basis for all Solvara access and revenue models, pending pricing-committee approval. Refine the 340B band and the Medicaid inflation-penalty out-year model as launch inputs firm up.
