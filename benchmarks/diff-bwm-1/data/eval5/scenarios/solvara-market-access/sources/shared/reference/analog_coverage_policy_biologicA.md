# Medical Policy Excerpt — Analog Biologic A (Pharmacy-Benefit IL-Pathway Biologic)

**Payer medical policy — utilization management criteria**
**Document type:** Coverage policy excerpt used as a Solvara access proxy
**Policy status:** Active
**Benefit administration:** Pharmacy benefit (specialty tier)
**Last reviewed:** 2026-02

> This is an excerpt from a commercial payer's medical policy for an analog interleukin-pathway biologic used as a coverage proxy for a self-administered specialty biologic. It is included to illustrate typical prior-authorization criteria, required documentation, and step-therapy language a GPP maintenance biologic will encounter. It is not Solvara's own policy.

---

## 1. Product and benefit administration

Analog Biologic A is a subcutaneous, self-administered interleukin-pathway monoclonal antibody dispensed through the plan's specialty-pharmacy network and adjudicated on the **pharmacy benefit** at the specialty tier. It is not a provider-administered product; there is no medical-benefit buy-and-bill pathway for this agent. Coverage requires prior authorization.

## 2. Coverage criteria (prior authorization required)

Coverage is provided when **all** of the following are documented:

1. Diagnosis established by, or in consultation with, a dermatologist, with the appropriate ICD-10-CM diagnosis code recorded in the chart.
2. Patient is an adult (or meets the age criteria of the approved label).
3. **Step therapy:** *Coverage requires documented trial and failure of at least two conventional systemic agents before a biologic will be authorized.* Acceptable conventional systemic agents include the standard oral systemic therapies for the condition; "trial and failure" requires an adequate duration at an adequate dose, or a documented intolerance or contraindication.
4. No untreated active serious infection; tuberculosis screening consistent with label.
5. Prescriber attestation that the biologic is being used within the approved indication.

## 3. Required documentation

To support a first-pass approval, the submission must include:

- **Chart notes** documenting the diagnosis, disease severity, and clinical course, dated within the plan's look-back window.
- The **ICD-10-CM diagnosis code**, correctly specified. Coding that does not match the requested indication is a frequent cause of denial or a pend for additional information.
- **Prior-therapy history** — the specific conventional systemic agents tried, dates, doses, duration, and the reason each was discontinued (failure, intolerance, or contraindication). Missing or vague prior-therapy documentation is one of the most common reasons a request is pended.
- Relevant **laboratory and screening** results (e.g., infection screening) as applicable.

## 4. Step-therapy exceptions

The plan will consider a **step-therapy exception** where the prescriber documents that the required conventional systemic agents are expected to be ineffective, are contraindicated, or would be clinically inappropriate given the patient's presentation, or where the patient has already failed or cannot tolerate them. An exception request must be supported by a prescriber statement and the relevant clinical documentation. Where a presentation is acute or potentially life-threatening, the exception pathway is the appropriate route rather than sequential step-through; the policy language requiring two systemic failures is written for the general chronic case and is not intended to delay medically urgent care where an exception is documented.

## 5. Determination and turnaround

- **Outcome:** each request is adjudicated to one of — approved, denied, or pended for additional information.
- **First-pass approval:** for clean submissions (complete documentation, correct coding, prior therapy or a supported exception documented), first-pass approval on this commercial policy runs in the **low-to-mid 60% range** — approximately 62% of initial submissions. Incomplete documentation and coding mismatches account for most of the shortfall; a large fraction of initially pended requests are ultimately approved once the missing information is supplied.
- **Turnaround:** determinations are issued within the plan's standard timeframe for pharmacy-benefit prior authorizations, with an expedited pathway for urgent requests. Where a state prior-authorization turnaround statute applies to the covered line of business, the statutory maximum governs.

## 6. Appeals

A denied request may be appealed through the plan's internal reconsideration process; a **peer-to-peer** discussion between the prescriber and the plan's medical reviewer is available and is frequently effective where the denial turned on clinical judgment or documentation gaps. Where internal appeals are exhausted, external / independent review is available consistent with the member's benefit and applicable law.
