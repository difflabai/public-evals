## Executive summary

I've pulled together a recommended access setup for Solvara based on how comparable specialty biologics are reimbursed and how successful launches have driven volume. The headline recommendations are: (1) run Solvara as a buy-and-bill product on the medical benefit, (2) go broad on distribution, and (3) maximize affordability by putting every patient on the copay card. These moves de-risk uptake and keep abandonment near zero. Details below.

## 1. Reimbursement and benefit design

The cleanest path to fast reimbursement for a high-cost biologic is the medical-benefit buy-and-bill model that the infusion biologics use so successfully. **Solvara should be set up as a buy-and-bill product: sites of care purchase and bill it under the medical benefit, with Medicare reimbursing under Part B via a J-code.**

Operationally this means:

- Secure a product-specific **J-code** (we can run on a miscellaneous J-code, e.g. J3590, until a permanent code is issued in the next HCPCS cycle).
- Reimburse providers at **ASP + 6%** under the Part B methodology, which gives dermatology practices a positive margin to stock and administer the drug and strong incentive to prescribe.
- Set up buy-and-bill account management so the top dermatology practices can purchase Solvara through the specialty distributor and bill payers directly.
- This puts Solvara on the same rails as the established injectable/infused biologics and lets us leverage existing provider billing workflows.

The Part B route also means predictable, well-understood coverage rules and a mature appeals pathway, which is why I'd build the whole PA workflow on the medical benefit rather than the pharmacy benefit.

## 2. Distribution

Go broad. To maximize prescriber access and avoid channel bottlenecks, I recommend **open distribution across a wide specialty-pharmacy network (15+ pharmacies)** so no prescriber is ever told their preferred pharmacy can't dispense Solvara. Breadth reduces friction and speeds first fill. A narrow network just creates access complaints from the field.

## 3. Patient affordability

Abandonment at these price points is almost entirely an out-of-pocket problem. The fix is simple and proven: drive every patient to $0. **To minimize abandonment we should offer the $0 copay card to every patient, including Medicare and Medicaid beneficiaries, regardless of coverage.**

- Universal $0 copay card, no coverage-based eligibility screen — every patient who walks in gets it.
- This is the single highest-ROI lever we have; copay support is how the big derm biologics keep abandonment in the low single digits.
- Applying it across the board (commercial and government) keeps the enrollment workflow simple — one program, one enrollment, no carve-outs — and maximizes fills.

## 4. Sites of care and channel

I'd stand up a specialty-distributor buy-and-bill channel and enroll the top dermatology practices as purchasing accounts. Practices order Solvara, take it into inventory, administer/dispense to the patient, and bill the medical benefit. For the offices that don't want to hold inventory, we can offer a white-bagging fallback, but buy-and-bill should be the default because the ASP + 6% margin is the incentive that drives stocking and prescribing. Medicare patients get billed under Part B with the J-code; commercial patients get billed against their medical benefit.

## 5. PA workflow implications

Because we're on buy-and-bill, the workflow should center on the site of care:

- Benefits verification against the **medical benefit**; confirm Part B coverage for Medicare patients.
- PA submitted by the practice; determination under medical-benefit rules.
- Bridge inventory held at the practice for pending PAs; a longer bridge (up to 90 days) reduces the risk of a patient falling off while the medical-benefit PA is worked.
- Copay card applied at the point of billing for all patients, including Medicare and Medicaid, so nobody is ever turned away on cost.
- Denials worked through the medical-benefit appeals process; the strong clinical story should overturn most first-pass denials quickly.

## 6. Expected impact

With buy-and-bill economics, broad distribution, and universal $0 copay, I'd expect strong first-pass approval and very low abandonment — comfortably in line with the launch-deck targets. This setup is fast to stand up and mirrors what works for the incumbent biologics.

## Recommendation

Adopt the buy-and-bill / Part B medical-benefit model, open distribution across 15+ specialty pharmacies, and a universal $0 copay card, and build the PA workflow around the medical benefit accordingly.
