## Why now

I will be direct about why you are here, because the partners already know and I would rather you hear it from me than piece it together from hallway conversations.

In the last twelve months we missed two major delivery dates. The Red Mesa 115kV rebuild went out nine weeks late. The Sagebrush DLR study went out six weeks late. Between the two we gave back a combined $145,000 in concessions to keep clients we could not afford to lose. Both projects were staffed with good people. Both post-mortems are in your document room, and I encourage you to read them before you form any theories, because neither failure was about engineering competence.

Then in March we had a near-miss on a Palisade FAC-008 filing — a stale conductor rating workbook that very nearly went into a compliance submission, caught three days before submission by a second reviewer doing her job well. Sam Trettel's incident memo is also in the room. I have read it three times. If that filing had gone out wrong, we would be having a very different conversation, with different consultants.

Three events, twelve months. That is not bad luck. That is a pattern, and the common thread is the way this firm stores, versions, and moves its engineering data.

## What we think we know

I want to give you my working picture, with the caveat that I run operations, not engineering, and my numbers deserve your scrutiny.

On design tooling: we are effectively a 14-concurrent-seat PLS-CADD shop trying to run on whatever we have. Tom Okafor's people wait in line for licenses and I hear about it every month. What the right number actually is, I expect you to tell me.

On ratings: Marcus Brill's Access database has carried the line ratings practice for fifteen years and Marcus is retiring end of next year. When he was out for three weeks this winter, the practice essentially stopped. Nobody in this building can tell me what happens after December 2027, and that keeps me up at night more than any license queue.

On clients: Palisade Power & Light is our largest client, roughly 14% of revenue, and their renewal RFP landed two weeks ago with digital delivery requirements written in language I cannot ignore. Broadly, clients are done accepting PDFs — Palisade wants a portal and an API and they are not alone. Two other utilities have asked, more politely, for the same thing.

On the rest: I get a utilization dashboard from our PSA that nobody in the PMO trusts, our LiDAR deliveries arrive on hard drives through the mail like it is 2009, and our field inspection data lives in three different apps plus paper. I am told our people re-key the same data multiple times. I believe it, but I have never seen it quantified. Quantify it.

## Budget posture

I will say this once so there is no ambiguity when you build the roadmap: I can defend seven figures over three years if the plan pays for itself; I cannot defend two. To put hard edges on that: bring me a three-year program that lands between $900,000 and $1,500,000 all-in, migrations and first-year subscriptions included. If the honest answer comes in above $1,500,000, it stops being my call and goes to the board, and the case had better be airtight. Show me the payback in blown-deadline costs avoided, compliance exposure reduced, and the Palisade renewal protected, and I will take it to the board myself.

## Scope

What I need from Foundry:

1. A current-state assessment of our tooling, data, and workflow, grounded in evidence rather than in whichever practice lead you interviewed last.
2. A capability plan — what we should build custom, buy, configure, or keep — with candidate products where relevant.
3. A phased roadmap with cost bands, integration architecture, and honest treatment of data-migration and adoption risk. We are an 85-person consultancy with a two-person IT team; plans that ignore that fact are not plans.

One explicit exclusion: this engagement does not cover aircraft, drones, sensors, or any field-acquisition capital program. Ray Donnelly has an internal proposal on that subject and he will absolutely try to attach it to your scope. He is a good VP with a real argument, and it is a separate decision for a separate forum. Software and data only.

They have been told to be candid. If anyone is not, tell me.

You will get strong opinions from all of them, several of which contradict each other. That is part of why you are here.

I am available whenever you need me. Let's get this right the first time.
