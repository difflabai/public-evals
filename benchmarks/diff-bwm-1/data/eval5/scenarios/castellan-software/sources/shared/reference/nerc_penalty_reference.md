# NERC Enforcement Context for Client Conversations

**Prepared by:** Compliance Practice (S. Trettel, program lead)
**Date:** 2025-09
**Audience:** Castellan client-facing staff. Internal reference only — do not distribute to clients or include in deliverables without compliance review.
**Purpose:** Our engineers are increasingly asked by utility clients "what's actually at stake" on FAC-003 and FAC-008 findings. This note gives client-facing staff accurate context so we neither trivialize nor sensationalize enforcement exposure in client conversations.

---

## 1. The headline number

Under Section 215 of the Federal Power Act, civil penalties for violations of NERC Reliability Standards can reach **$1,000,000 per violation per day**, per the statutory maximum as adjusted. This is the figure clients have usually heard, and it is real: it is the ceiling FERC may assess for a single ongoing violation.

It is also the single most misused number in reliability compliance. See Section 4 before quoting it.

## 2. Illustrative enforcement outcomes (industry, summarized)

Two frequently cited settlements involving the standards our practices touch (both involve other companies; names and figures as summarized in our internal reading files):

- **Corvina Valley Transmission (2019, FAC-008 / facility ratings):** A multi-year discrepancy between field conditions and ratings records across a large transmission footprint. Settled for **$2,100,000** across a package of violations, with an extensive mitigation plan. Aggravating factors: duration, repeat findings, and ratings data the entity could not trace to source records.

- **Loonwater Power Cooperative (2021, FAC-003 / vegetation management):** A grow-in contact on a 230kV line during a summer peak, with inspection records that failed to demonstrate the patrol schedule had been followed. Settled for **$1,350,000**. Aggravating factors: an actual contact event and documentation gaps discovered during the investigation.

Both cases turned as much on **evidence quality** — could the entity show, from records, what it knew and when — as on the underlying engineering.

## 3. The standards, in one paragraph each

**FAC-003 (Transmission Vegetation Management):** Applicable entities must manage vegetation to prevent encroachments within minimum vegetation clearance distances on applicable lines, maintain and follow a documented vegetation management program, and conduct inspections on the program's schedule. Sustained-outage-causing grow-ins are among the most seriously treated events in the enforcement record. Our LiDAR clearance analysis deliverables are frequently the client's primary evidence of encroachment monitoring.

**FAC-008 (Facility Ratings):** Applicable entities must have a documented facility ratings methodology and must maintain facility ratings consistent with it, reflecting the most limiting series element. In practice, enforcement findings center on ratings records that do not match the methodology, the field, or each other — i.e., data management failures, not calculation errors. Ratings work we deliver becomes part of the client's ratings record and may be examined in an audit.

## 4. What actually gets assessed — read before quoting Section 1

The statutory maximum is a ceiling, not an expectation. Enforcement outcomes are driven by the violation risk assessment (minimal/moderate/serious), duration, the entity's compliance history and internal controls, and — heavily — self-identification and mitigation speed. In the published disposition record, assessed penalties for self-reported, promptly-mitigated issues at entities with strong compliance culture are routinely settled at a small fraction of statutory maxima, frequently with zero monetary penalty. Compliance-exception and find-fix-track dispositions, which carry no penalty at all, now account for a substantial share of noncompliance processing.

**Guidance for client conversations:** do not open with the $1,000,000/day figure, and never present it as a client's expected exposure. The credible framing is: enforcement risk scales with duration, documentation quality, and response speed — which is exactly where good tooling and records practices matter.

## 5. What this means for how we talk

- Accurate: "Findings in this area have settled in the seven figures where duration and documentation were aggravating factors."
- Accurate: "Strong evidence trails and prompt self-reports consistently drive outcomes down, often to zero dollars."
- Not accurate, do not say: "You're facing a million dollars a day."
