## Executive summary

Castellan currently procures 100% of its airborne LiDAR acquisition from three third-party flight vendors, at charter day-rates that have risen every season since 2022. This proposal recommends that Castellan establish an in-house unmanned aircraft systems (UAS) capability: two survey-grade UAS platforms with LiDAR sensor payloads, two FAA Part 107 certificated pilots, and a training and maintenance program. **Total first-year program cost: $450,000 all-in.** The program pays for itself against helicopter and fixed-wing charter spend in under three seasons on the corridor segments suitable for UAS acquisition, while giving Castellan schedule control, same-day data custody, and a differentiated story in every veg-management pursuit.

## The problem: we rent our most strategic capability

Every line-mile we deliver starts with an aircraft we do not control. Consequences:

- **Schedule hostage-taking.** Our vendors serve larger customers first. Weather windows get burned waiting in their queue, and reflight requests go to the back of the line.
- **Cost escalation.** Blended charter costs are up roughly 30% since 2022. We pass some through; the rest erodes practice margin.
- **Data custody lag.** Deliverables come back to us on couriered hard drives on the vendor's timeline. With our own aircraft, the sensor lands and the data is ours the same afternoon.
- **No pursuit differentiation.** Competitors are beginning to advertise organic acquisition capability. We currently sell analysis only.

| **Total, year 1** | | **$450,000** |

Ongoing years: pilot compensation share, maintenance, insurance, and consumables, estimated at $145,000/yr.

## Payback illustration

Assumptions: UAS-suitable segments substitute for charter acquisition at current blended day rates; utilization ramps over two seasons.

| Year | Charter spend avoided | Program cost | Net |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $120,000 | $450,000 | ($330,000) |
| 2 | $210,000 | $145,000 | +$65,000 |
| 3 | $240,000 | $145,000 | +$95,000 |

Cumulative program breakeven occurs mid-Year 3; every season after that is margin. These figures are deliberately conservative — they exclude reflight savings, pursuit-win uplift, and the schedule value of controlling our own weather windows.

## Strategic fit

The 2024 strategic plan named "differentiated data acquisition" as a growth theme. This program operationalizes it. UAS acquisition will not replace all vendor flights — long-corridor programs will remain fixed-wing charter work for the foreseeable future — but it converts the short-segment, reflight, and rapid-response work we currently buy at premium rates into work we perform at marginal cost.

## Scope note

For clarity in the current planning cycle: this proposal addresses acquisition capacity, not the data management gap — point-cloud handling is out of scope here. Storage, cataloging, and delivery-pipeline questions for LiDAR data exist regardless of whether the sensor is on our aircraft or a vendor's, and are being examined separately.

## Risks and mitigations

- **Regulatory:** BVLOS (beyond visual line of sight) waivers remain case-by-case; Year-1 operations plan assumes VLOS segments only. Mitigation: target waiver application in Year 2 with an established safety record.
- **Utilization:** Program economics depend on flying the aircraft. Mitigation: pre-commit UAS-suitable segments from two flagship clients before purchase; both account teams have verbal interest.
- **Key personnel:** Two pilots is minimum viable redundancy. Mitigation: cross-train one analyst to Part 107 in Year 1.
- **Insurance:** Aviation liability uplift is quoted and included above.

## Recommendation

Approve $450,000 in the FY2027 capital plan for the Castellan UAS Acquisition Program, with procurement beginning October 2026 to be flight-ready for the 2027 season. The practice is prepared to present vendor quotes and the detailed operations plan at the next capital review.
