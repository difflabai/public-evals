## Section 2 — Where We Stand

Castellan Grid Consulting enters FY2024 from a position of strength. Today we are a 72-person firm with deep, defensible expertise across transmission line ratings, LiDAR-based vegetation management, facility ratings compliance, and line design. Our client roster spans investor-owned utilities, generation and transmission cooperatives, and public power districts across the Mountain West and Plains, and our win rate on competitive pursuits has exceeded 60% for two consecutive years.

Our people are our product. Retention among senior engineering staff remains above industry norms, and our principal-led delivery model continues to differentiate us against national firms that staff junior and bill senior.

The board has set a clear financial course: **grow firm revenue to $21M by FY2027** while holding EBITDA margin at or above 18%. Management believes this target is achievable through organic growth in our existing service lines, without acquisition and without geographic expansion beyond our three offices.

## Section 4 — Strategic Initiatives (FY2024–FY2026)

**Initiative 1 — Deepen the DLR franchise.** Dynamic line rating studies are moving from pilot curiosity to procurement mainstream. Castellan will expand DLR study capacity and publish two thought-leadership pieces per year to hold our first-mover position.

**Initiative 2 — Institutionalize the compliance practice.** FAC-003 and FAC-008 program support is our stickiest revenue. We will grow the compliance team and pursue multi-year program-support retainers rather than one-time assessments.

**Initiative 3 — Grow Kansas City.** The KC office reached breakeven in FY2023. We will add two to four billable staff there by FY2026 to serve the SPP-footprint client base.

**Initiative 4 — Evaluate ERP platforms in FY2025.** Our back-office systems have not kept pace with firm growth. Finance will lead a structured evaluation of ERP platforms in FY2025, with a decision gate at the FY2025 mid-year review. (See Appendix C, vendor long list.)

**Initiative 5 — Pilot cloud storage for LiDAR deliverables.** The vegetation management practice has completed a successful pilot of a commercial cloud storage account for LiDAR point-cloud deliveries. With the pilot proven, management considers the field-data storage question resolved for the planning horizon; the account will be scaled as flight volumes grow.

## Appendix A — SWOT Summary

| | Helpful | Harmful |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | **Strengths:** principal-led delivery; DLR first-mover credibility; compliance retainer base; low senior attrition | **Weaknesses:** key-person concentration in the ratings practice; aging internal tooling; back-office systems below firm scale |
| **External** | **Opportunities:** FERC Order 881 driving AAR/DLR demand; wildfire-driven vegetation management spend; storm-hardening federal funding | **Threats:** national firms moving down-market; utility insourcing of ratings work; wage inflation for PLS-CADD-fluent engineers |

## Planning assumptions (Section 2.3, reproduced)

- Headcount grows from 72 to approximately 80 by end of FY2026, concentrated in billable roles.
- No material capital expenditures beyond routine IT refresh are contemplated in this plan.
- Software spend is treated as a cost line to be managed, not a strategic lever.
